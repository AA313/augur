# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary user is a dreamer: someone who has woken from a dream that seemed to arrive before the thing it described, and who wants a way to capture it and later prove it came first. They arrive curious and a little uncertain, often having felt dismissed when they tried to tell someone after the fact. They are not looking to be told their dreams are magic; they are looking for a way to make the claim honest and checkable.

The rigor exists to satisfy a harder second reader: the skeptic and the outside observer. A sealed dream has to hold up to a stranger who assumes it is coincidence or hindsight. The design speaks to the dreamer, but every claim it makes must survive that scrutiny, which is why the proof, the misses, and the verifier are always in reach.

## Product Purpose

Oneiratory is a registry for dreams and the rare moments they seem to arrive early. A person records a dream, seals it so its exact wording is cryptographically fixed to a moment in time, and later proves the dream was written before the event it seemed to foretell. Around that core sit a private journal (the Vault), a public ledger of resolved predictions (the Registry), an anonymous discussion board (the Commons), and a standalone proof verifier.

Success is a growing, honest record: dreams sealed in advance, resolved in public, with the misses shown next to the hits. The point is not to prove that dreams predict the future. It is to refuse to let the question stay unanswerable, by making every claim falsifiable, timestamped, and checkable by strangers.

## Positioning

An instrument, not an oracle. Oneiratory takes no position on whether dreams foresee anything; a seal proves only anteriority, that a specific text existed, unchanged, before a moment in time. That honesty, and the discipline of declaring the success condition before the event, is the one claim every screen reinforces.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: seal a dream. Secondary CTA, for the visitor not ready to seal or not yet convinced the mechanism is real: verify a proof themselves.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: prove you dreamed it first.
- Belief ladder: everyone has felt a dream seem to land early, then dismissed it. The problem is that memory softens and no one can tell whether the dream really came first. That gap is exactly what a timestamped, tamper-proof seal closes. Oneiratory is an instrument, not a psychic app: it shows its misses, and anyone can check the proof. So the honest move is to seal the next dream you cannot shake tonight.
- Proof on hand: none yet. This is a working prototype; the cryptographic seal is real, the timestamp anchor is still simulated. As real anchored proofs and resolved Registry entries accumulate, they become the proof.

## Brand Personality

Dreamy, calm, and reflective, with the honesty of an instrument and the precision of a cryptographic seal. The voice invites people to share the wonder of their dreams without ever flattering the belief that dreams are magic. It is serious and a little mystical, the tone of a rigorous person keeping an honest record in the small hours, not a product being marketed. Warm and unhurried on the surface, exact underneath. Copy stays plain and never overclaims. House rule: no em dashes anywhere in user-facing text.

## Anti-references

- Anything that looks AI-generated. This is the primary one. Gradient-filled pill buttons, glassmorphism and backdrop-blur cards, pastel radial-gradient hero washes, glossy "dreamcore" polish: the trendy 2026 AI-app look. It makes a serious project read as a toy and puts real users off. The redesign into a faded old-web instrument exists specifically to escape it.
- Psychic and occult woo: crystal balls, tarot, moon-and-stars mysticism, fortune-teller framing. Oneiratory is explicitly not a psychic app.
- Crypto and web3 hype: neon-on-black, "blockchain-verified" badges, techbro urgency, despite there being a real cryptographic seal underneath.
- Clinical SaaS coldness: sterile lab-white dashboards and enterprise-tool sameness that strip out the wonder.

## Design Principles

- Look like an instrument, not an app. The design must read as a serious, faintly mystical old-web record-keeper, never as a fashionable AI product. Demonstrate the mechanism; do not sell it. If an element implies Oneiratory knows the future, or looks AI-generated, it is wrong.
- Honesty over hype. Misses sit next to hits, and the verifier is always within reach. Credibility is the product, so the design never hides the unflattering parts.
- Wonder you can trust. Keep the faded, uncanny, small-hours mood, but always anchor it to something exact and checkable; the awe and the rigor share every page.
- Two registers, split by purpose. The Instrument (homepage, Registry, Verifier, Vault) is serious and evidentiary; the Commons is an anonymous board, loose and welcoming. Never let the Commons' looseness dilute the Instrument's credibility.
- Trust and anonymity are the product. Private Vault entries and anonymous posts are never exposed or sold, and the design makes that safety legible rather than asking for blind faith.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AAA where feasible, holding at least AA everywhere. The faded, low-contrast old-web palette is the risk here: "faint" must be spent only on chrome (borders, counts, meta), while reading text stays at the dark ink token and hit/miss labels stay fully legible. Verify muted text against the exact panel behind it, not the token name. Keep full keyboard operability and visible focus across the Vault, seal flow, Registry, Commons, and verifier, and honor prefers-reduced-motion for any residual animation (the seal ring).
