AUGUR is a place to record a dream and prove you wrote it down before the event it seemed to foretell. You write the dream, say in advance what would count as it coming true, and seal it: its exact words are locked to this moment in time. If the event later happens, the proof is already waiting, and anyone can check it themselves.

AUGUR takes no side on whether dreams can see ahead. It simply keeps an honest record, with the misses shown next to the hits. But for the first time it turns a question people have only ever believed or dismissed into one that can actually be tested, and if a large enough honest record ever showed these dreams landing more often than chance can explain, the implications for how we understand the mind, memory, and time would be profound.

The questionwhat are we actually trying to find out

A premonition only counts if it was written down first.

Everyone has felt it: a dream that seems to land days before the event it described. But by the time the event arrives, memory has softened, and no one can tell whether the dream truly came first or whether the mind quietly rewrote it after. That gap is where every claim of foresight collapses.

AUGUR is built to close that gap and to answer one narrow, testable question: did the words of this dream provably exist, unchanged, before the event it seemed to foretell? Nothing more mystical than that, and nothing less rigorous. We take no position on why a dream might arrive early. We only make sure that when one does, it can be proven, and when it does not, that is on the record too.

How the seal worksthe mechanism, in four movements

  1. Write the dream, and declare the target

    You record the dream and, in advance, state plainly what would count as it coming true. The condition is sealed alongside the dream. You are calling the shot before you take it, the same discipline a scientist uses registering a hypothesis before the experiment.

  2. Seal it

    The exact text and its condition are run through SHA‑256, a standard, one-way method that turns any text into a short, unique fingerprint (the same technique that secures passwords and banking). Change a single character and the fingerprint changes completely. That fingerprint is stamped to a public timeline, so the words are now locked to this moment and cannot be moved.

  3. Wait

    The dream stays private and unread while the world turns. Nothing about it can be edited, and its sealing time is fixed for good.

  4. Reveal, and let a stranger verify

    When the event arrives, you unseal the dream. Anyone can recompute the fingerprint themselves and confirm the words existed, untouched, before the event. Not on our word. On the arithmetic.

Why this is an instrument, not a psychic app
  • You declare the target before the shot. The oldest trick in prophecy is the vague dream stretched afterwards to fit whatever happened. AUGUR forbids it: the success condition is committed up front and sealed.
  • The misses are public. A ledger that only shows hits is advertising. The Registry shows the failures next to the successes, because the failures are what make the successes mean anything.
  • A stranger can check it. Every proof stands on a recomputable hash and a fixed timestamp. You never have to trust AUGUR, or the dreamer. You can verify it yourself, offline.
What a record like this could be worththe case for keeping it honest

Scattered anecdotes have never been able to settle whether dreams can reach into what is coming. The evidence was always unprovable: a story retold after the fact, with no way to date it and no way to count the times it failed.

A sealed registry changes what is possible to study. Every entry is a pre-registered prediction with a declared condition, a fixed timestamp, and a recorded outcome, hit or miss. Kept at scale, that stops being a pile of stories and becomes a dataset: falsifiable, time-anchored, and open to anyone who wants to measure it.

For researchers of dreams, memory, and cognition, that opens real questions. How often do pre-registered dream predictions actually resolve as hits, against what chance alone would give? Do certain kinds of dream, or certain dreamers, resolve more often than others? How much of memory’s certainty about a dream is built only after the event? AUGUR cannot answer these on its own, but it can supply the one thing this question has always lacked: honest, dated, verifiable data. Anonymised submissions may be used in research and cited in scholarly work.
